Lifecycle navigation

Group screens by the stages of running a buy, not generic software categories. Organisers immediately know where to go.

2

Action-first overview

The home screen answers “what needs me now?” before showing charts or passive information.

3

Fit-for-purpose tabs

Orders use records, dispatch uses readiness queues, testing uses stages, and settings use structured forms.

4

Peps visual continuity

Keep the existing S&P rail, navy/blue hierarchy, Inter type, soft-grey canvas, and friendly rounded controls.

How the system should feel

Calm under pressureDense enough for real operations, but never visually noisy.
Guided, not restrictivePrioritise the next action while preserving access to every advanced tool.
Domain-specificUse parcels, COAs, payment windows, testing rounds, and dispatch readiness as first-class concepts.
Consistent with PepsThe organiser area should feel like the professional working side of the existing website.

What I would avoid

  • A kanban board on every page
  • Generic CRM language such as leads and deals
  • Large decorative charts without an action
  • A completely separate visual identity
  • Too many simultaneous navigation levels
