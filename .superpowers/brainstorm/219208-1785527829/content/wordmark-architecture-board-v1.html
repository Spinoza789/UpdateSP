Wordmark architecture board

A more literal translation of the three references you selected. Each option keeps SALT&PEPS as the primary asset and borrows the reference's actual construction: letter weight, case, curve behavior, and spacing. These are directional proxies for approval, not final artwork.

01 / Frank architecture

title case · blunt geometric mass · no ornament

Source anatomy
SALT&PEPS construction target

One dense geometric voice. The distinction must come from custom terminals, width, and ampersand geometry, not from a colored icon or extra graphic.

What this means for SALT&PEPS: a large, compact wordmark with enough visual mass to stand on its own. It is the cleanest route if the goal is professional, memorable, and visibly authored without looking “scientific.” The open question is whether the brand can relax the existing all-caps convention for a title-case lockup.

02 / Pivotal architecture

lowercase · rounded weight · one organic rise

Source anatomy
SALT&PEPS construction target

A rounded lowercase wordmark with a rising gesture integrated into the first “p” zone. The accent is part of the lettering, not a separate symbol.

What this means for SALT&PEPS: the most human and healthcare-adjacent route. It can carry a small descriptor beneath the name later, but it risks feeling too soft if the custom forms are not kept broad and disciplined. The current all-caps contract would need an explicit exception for this direction.

03 / Yango architecture

lowercase · engineered curves · controlled color

Source anatomy
SALT&PEPS construction target

A custom lowercase alphabet with consistent curve logic, open counters, and a blue range. The identity comes from owning the letter system, not adding a molecule or badge.

What this means for SALT&PEPS: the most contemporary and system-led route. It offers the strongest opportunity to custom-draw the ampersand and both “s” terminals, but the gradient would need to be treated as an optional presentation layer because the production contract requires flat, single-color variants.
