---
name: Product refund reporting decisions
description: Financial boundaries for reviewed product-only refunds in supplier profit reporting.
---

Customer refunds reduce attributable product sales, not incurred supplier costs. Shipping, fees and other non-product refund amounts stay outside product gross profit. Do not infer a supplier credit from a customer refund.

**Why:** Whole-order refunds mix different charges, and returning money to a customer does not establish that the supplier returned its product cost.

**How to apply:** Require completed refund evidence and an explicit attributed product review. Keep unresolved refunds excluded rather than subtracting their whole payout.

Shared shipments require member-level product quantities and sales frozen with the supplier submission, not current order financials, even when those current values reconcile to the supplier's original aggregate. Do not allocate a refund using a weighted shared selling price or today's catalogue.

**Why:** Members in the same shipment may have bought identical products at different prices. Offsetting post-submission edits (for example, $50/$50 becoming $60/$40) preserve the aggregate while changing attribution. Aggregate reconciliation does not establish historical member evidence.

**How to apply:** Leave missing or inconsistent historical attribution unavailable. Preserve review evidence independently; do not rewrite purchased goods or supplier receipts to make the report reconcile.

Refund adjustments restate the original supplier-submission period rather than creating cash movements on the refund date.

**Why:** This report measures product gross profit by supplier submission, not cash flow.

**How to apply:** Keep date filters and daily buckets on the existing supplier-submission basis and disclose the treatment in the report.
